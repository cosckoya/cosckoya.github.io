# :material-movie-open: Horror Cinema

Horror cinema gave the monster a face and a franchise. The slashers of the 1970s and 80s — Freddy, Jason, Michael Myers, Leatherface — turned the killer into a brand, with masks, trademarks, and sequels. Modern horror added the supernatural icons: Pennywise the dancing clown, Samara of the Ring, the Babadook. These creatures are not folklore; they are copyrights. But they are the monsters our generation grew up with, and they follow the same rules as the old ones: don't go alone, don't trust the mask, and never answer the phone.

!!! tip "Start Here"
    Start with the four slasher kings: *Halloween* (1978), *Friday the 13th* (1980), *A Nightmare on Elm Street* (1984), and *The Texas Chain Saw Massacre* (1974). Then the modern icons: *It* (1990/2017), *The Ring* (2002), *The Babadook* (2014). The genre is a language — learn the classics first.

## :material-knife: The Slashers

### :material-knife: Freddy Krueger

**Origin:** A Nightmare on Elm Street, 1984 · **Type:** Dream killer · **Source:** Wes Craven

Freddy Krueger is the monster of dreams: a burned child-killer who returns as a dream-demon and kills teenagers inside their nightmares — where the deaths are real. His signature is the bladed glove, his lair is the boiler room, and his power is that you cannot stay awake forever. The original film is a genuine horror classic; the sequels turned Freddy into a comedian, and the 2010 remake failed. Freddy is the monster of the vulnerable hour: the moment you close your eyes.

**Where to learn more:** [Wikipedia: Freddy Krueger](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Freddy_Krueger) · [A Nightmare on Elm Street](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/A_Nightmare_on_Elm_Street) · [Wes Craven](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wes_Craven)

### :material-axe: Jason Voorhees

**Origin:** Friday the 13th, 1980 · **Type:** Slasher · **Source:** Victor Miller, Sean S. Cunningham

Jason Voorhees is the monster of the lake: a child who drowned at Camp Crystal Lake, who returns as an unstoppable, masked killer. The hockey mask came in Part 3; the franchise ran for 12 films plus a remake and a TV series. Jason is the archetypal "final girl survivor" foil — he walks, he doesn't run, he does not stop, and he has no dialogue. He is the monster of the place: the camp with the bad history.

**Where to learn more:** [Wikipedia: Jason Voorhees](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jason_Voorhees) · [Friday the 13th (franchise)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Friday_the_13th_(franchise)) · [The hockey mask](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jason_Voorhees#Hockey_mask)

### :material-ghost: Michael Myers

**Origin:** Halloween, 1978 · **Type:** Silent killer · **Source:** John Carpenter

Michael Myers is "The Shape": a silent, white-masked killer who murdered his sister as a child and returns to his hometown 15 years later to kill again. John Carpenter's *Halloween* defined the slasher genre, and Michael is its purest form — no motive, no dialogue, no supernatural explanation (until the sequels added one). He is the monster of the suburb: the neighbor who was always wrong.

**Where to learn more:** [Wikipedia: Michael Myers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Michael_Myers_(Halloween)) · [Halloween (1978)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Halloween_(1978_film)) · [John Carpenter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Carpenter)

### :material-saw-blade: Leatherface

**Origin:** The Texas Chain Saw Massacre, 1974 · **Type:** Cannibal · **Source:** Tobe Hooper

Leatherface is the chainsaw-wielding cannibal of the Sawyer family, wearing a mask made of human skin. The 1974 film is one of the most influential horror movies ever made — raw, documentary-style, and genuinely disturbing. Leatherface is the monster of the rural: the isolated farmhouse where the family dinner is not what it seems. He is the slasher as family business.

**Where to learn more:** [Wikipedia: Leatherface](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leatherface) · [The Texas Chain Saw Massacre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Texas_Chain_Saw_Massacre) · [Tobe Hooper](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tobe_Hooper)

### :material-dolly: Chucky

**Origin:** Child's Play, 1988 · **Type:** Possessed doll · **Source:** Don Mancini

Chucky is a Good Guy doll possessed by the soul of serial killer Charles Lee Ray, who keeps killing and trading bodies across a franchise that ran from horror to comedy-horror to TV. The possessed-doll premise is the modern update of the automaton: the toy that is secretly a killer. Chucky is the monster of the nursery — the thing in the toy box.

**Where to learn more:** [Wikipedia: Chucky](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chucky_(character)) · [Child's Play (1988)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Child%27s_Play_(1988_film)) · [Chucky (TV series)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chucky_(TV_series))

## :material-ghost: The Supernatural Icons

### :material-ghost: Pennywise

**Origin:** It, 1990 / 2017 · **Type:** Shapeshifting monster · **Source:** Stephen King

Pennywise the Dancing Clown is the preferred form of "It," an ancient cosmic entity that awakens every 27 years in Derry, Maine, to feed on children's fears — taking whatever shape terrifies its victim most. Stephen King's novel is a thousand-page exploration of childhood fear; the 2017 film made Pennywise (Bill Skarsgård) a global icon. It is the monster of the sewer: the thing that lives under the town, waiting.

**Where to learn more:** [Wikipedia: Pennywise](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pennywise_(It)) · [It (novel)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/It_(novel)) · [It (2017 film)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/It_(2017_film))

### :material-television: Samara / Sadako

**Origin:** The Ring, 2002 (Ringu 1998) · **Type:** Curse ghost · **Source:** Koji Suzuki, Hideo Nakata

Samara (Sadako in the Japanese original) is the ghost of a murdered girl whose curse travels through a videotape: watch it, and you die in seven days. The Ring (2002) was the film that introduced J-horror to the West, and its image — the girl with hair over her face crawling out of a TV — is one of horror's most iconic. She is the monster of media: the message you cannot unsee.

**Where to learn more:** [Wikipedia: Samara Morgan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Samara_Morgan) · [The Ring (2002)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Ring_(2002_film)) · [Ringu (1998)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ring_(film))

### :material-book: The Babadook

**Origin:** The Babadook, 2014 · **Type:** Shadow entity · **Source:** Jennifer Kent

The Babadook is the monster of a pop-up book that comes to life: a tall, shadowy figure in a top hat that terrorizes a widowed mother and her son. It is grief and depression given form — the film is a masterpiece of horror as metaphor. The Babadook is the monster of the mind: the thing you are carrying that you cannot name, until it names itself.

**Where to learn more:** [Wikipedia: The Babadook](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Babadook) · [The Babadook (film)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Babadook) · [Jennifer Kent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jennifer_Kent)

### :material-skull: Pinhead

**Origin:** Hellraiser, 1987 · **Type:** Cenobite · **Source:** Clive Barker

Pinhead is the leader of the Cenobites — beings from another dimension who appear when the Lament Configuration puzzle box is solved, offering "pleasure and pain." Clive Barker's *Hellraiser* is gothic body horror, and Pinhead's design — a pale face studded with pins — is one of horror's most striking images. He is the monster of the bargain: the wish you should not have made.

**Where to learn more:** [Wikipedia: Pinhead](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pinhead_(Hellraiser)) · [Hellraiser (1987)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hellraiser_(1987_film)) · [Clive Barker](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clive_Barker)

## :lucide-dog: The Universal & Classic

### :material-dog-side: The Wolf Man

**Origin:** The Wolf Man, 1941 · **Type:** Werewolf · **Source:** Universal, Curt Siodmak

Larry Talbot becomes the Wolf Man when bitten — and the 1941 Universal film codified the werewolf rules still used today: the full moon, the pentagram on the palm, the silver bullet, the rhyme. It is the werewolf as tragedy: a man who knows what he becomes and cannot stop it. The Wolf Man is the monster of the moon: the animal inside, rising on schedule.

**Where to learn more:** [Wikipedia: The Wolf Man](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Wolf_Man_(1941_film)) · [Lon Chaney Jr.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lon_Chaney_Jr.) · [Universal Monsters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Universal_Monsters)

### :material-water: The Creature from the Black Lagoon

**Origin:** Creature from the Black Lagoon, 1954 · **Type:** Gill-man · **Source:** Universal, Jack Arnold

The Creature (Gill-man) is an amphibious humanoid that lives in the Amazon and falls for a female scientist — the last of the classic Universal monsters, and the one that most invites sympathy. The 1954 film was shot in true 3D and is remembered for the underwater ballet of the creature swimming beneath the heroine. He is the monster of the deep river: the prehistoric thing that never went extinct.

**Where to learn more:** [Wikipedia: Gill-man](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gill-man) · [Creature from the Black Lagoon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Creature_from_the_Black_Lagoon) · [Jack Arnold](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jack_Arnold_(director))

### :material-baby-face: The Bride of Frankenstein

**Origin:** Bride of Frankenstein, 1935 · **Type:** Created woman · **Source:** James Whale

The Bride is the female creature made to be the Monster's companion — she exists for one film, screams at her intended, and is destroyed in the explosion of the laboratory. Her image — the towering white-streaked hairdo — is iconic. The Bride is the monster of the made: a woman created for a purpose she rejects. She is the creation that refuses its role.

**Where to learn more:** [Wikipedia: Bride of Frankenstein](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bride_of_Frankenstein) · [Elsa Lanchester](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elsa_Lanchester) · [James Whale](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_Whale)
