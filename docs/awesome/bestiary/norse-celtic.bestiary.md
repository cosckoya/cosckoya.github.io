# :lucide-moon: Norse & Celtic

Cold iron, deep water, and the end of the world. Norse mythology gives us monsters with a deadline: Fenrir is destined to swallow Odin, Jörmungandr to drown the world. Celtic tradition trades apocalypse for liminal dread — wailing spirits, water-horses that drown riders, and the undead who were never properly buried. Different flavors, same rule: don't go in the water, don't answer the wail, don't trust the horse.

!!! tip "Start Here"
    The **Poetic Edda** and **Prose Edda** are the primary sources for Norse monsters — public domain, free at Sacred Texts. For Celtic folklore, the *Book of Invasions* and the folklore collections of W.Y. Evans-Wentz are the classic starting points.

## :lucide-dog: The Wolves

### :lucide-dog: Fenrir

**Origin:** Norse mythology · **Type:** Monstrous wolf · **Source:** Poetic Edda, Prose Edda

Son of Loki and the giantess Angrboða, Fenrir grew so vast the gods bound him with a magical chain forged from six impossible things — the sound of a cat's footfall, a woman's beard, the roots of a mountain, a bear's sinews, a fish's breath, and a bird's spittle. One god lost a hand in the process. At Ragnarök, Fenrir breaks free, swallows Odin, and is slain by Víðarr, who tears his jaw apart. He is fate you cannot outrun, wearing a wolf's shape.

**Where to learn more:** [Norse Mythology: Fenrir](https://norse-mythology.org/fenrir/) · [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fenrir) · [Poetic Edda (Völuspá)](https://www.sacred-texts.com/neu/poe/poe03.htm)

## :material-snake: The Serpents

### :material-snake: Jörmungandr

**Origin:** Norse mythology · **Type:** World serpent · **Source:** Prose Edda

The Midgard Serpent, another child of Loki, was thrown into the sea where it grew until it encircled the entire world, biting its own tail — the ouroboros given cosmic scale. At Ragnarök it surfaces, poisons the sky, and kills Thor; Thor kills it in return and dies from its venom. A creature so large it defines the boundaries of the map. The Kraken is sometimes claimed as a later, seafaring echo of this archetype.

**Where to learn more:** [Norse Mythology: Jörmungandr](https://norse-mythology.org/jormungandr/) · [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/J%C3%B6rmungandr) · [Prose Edda](https://www.sacred-texts.com/neu/pre/)

### :material-snake: Níðhöggr

**Origin:** Norse mythology · **Type:** Corpse-eating dragon · **Source:** Poetic Edda, Prose Edda

Níðhöggr gnaws at the roots of Yggdrasil, the world tree, feeding on the corpses of the dead and endlessly trying to destroy the structure that holds reality together. In some accounts a squirrel named Ratatoskr carries insults between Níðhöggr and the eagle at the top of the tree — cosmic gossip as world-destroying force. He is entropy with teeth.

**Where to learn more:** [Norse Mythology: Níðhöggr](https://norse-mythology.org/nidhogg/) · [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/N%C3%AD%C3%B0h%C3%B6ggr) · [Völuspá](https://www.sacred-texts.com/neu/poe/poe03.htm)

## :lucide-fish: The Deep Ones

### :lucide-fish: The Kraken

**Origin:** Norse folklore (later Scandinavian) · **Type:** Giant sea creature · **Source:** Pontoppidan, medieval sagas

The Kraken was described by 18th-century bishop Erik Pontoppidan as a creature so enormous it was mistaken for an island; sailors who landed on it learned their mistake when it dove. Older Norse sagas mention the *hafgufa* — a similar sea-monster that lured fish into its mouth. Modern versions have made it a giant squid, thanks to Jules Verne and later cinema. The original was bigger: it was geography with a grudge.

**Where to learn more:** [Wikipedia: Kraken](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kraken) · [Sacred Texts: Natural History of Norway](https://www.sacred-texts.com/earth/za/index.htm) · [Jules Verne's 20,000 Leagues](https://www.gutenberg.org/ebooks/164)

## :lucide-skull: The Undead

### :lucide-skull: Draugr

**Origin:** Norse mythology · **Type:** Reanimated corpse · **Source:** Grettis Saga, Eyrbyggja Saga

The draugr is a corpse that refuses to stay dead — it guards its burial mound, hoards its grave goods, swells to enormous size, and can crush intruders to death. Some draugar could walk through solid rock, shapeshift, and curse the living. The sagas treat them as a practical problem: dig up the body, behead it, burn it, or rebury it. They are the original "the grave was not the end."

**Where to learn more:** [Norse Mythology: Draugr](https://norse-mythology.org/draugr/) · [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Draugr) · [Grettis Saga](https://www.sagadb.org/grettis_saga.en)

### :lucide-ghost: The Banshee

**Origin:** Irish folklore · **Type:** Wailing spirit · **Source:** Celtic tradition

The *bean sí* ("woman of the mound") is a spirit whose keening warns of an imminent death in the family. She is not the cause — she is the announcement. Some versions describe her washing bloodstained clothes at a ford, a motif older than the banshee herself. Modern horror reframes her scream as an attack; traditional folklore treats her as a mournful herald you cannot outrun.

**Where to learn more:** [Wikipedia: Banshee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Banshee) · [Sacred Texts: Celtic folklore](https://www.sacred-texts.com/neu/celt/) · [Evans-Wentz: Fairy-Faith in Celtic Countries](https://www.sacred-texts.com/neu/celt/ffcc/index.htm)

## :material-water: The Water Spirits

### :material-water: Kelpie

**Origin:** Scottish folklore · **Type:** Water-horse · **Source:** Celtic tradition

The kelpie appears as a beautiful horse by a river or loch. Lure a child onto its back and its skin becomes adhesive; it gallops into the water and drowns its rider. In some tellings it is a shapeshifter that takes human form. It is the perfect trap: beauty, trust, and a ride that ends underwater. Every "don't trust the strange horse" warning in folklore traces back to something like it.

**Where to learn more:** [Wikipedia: Kelpie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kelpie) · [Sacred Texts: Scottish folklore](https://www.sacred-texts.com/neu/celt/kilt/index.htm) · [The Water-Horse tradition](https://www.sacred-texts.com/neu/celt/effc/effc05.htm)

### :material-waves: The Selkie & Seal-Folk

**Origin:** Celtic (Scottish & Irish) folklore · **Type:** Shapeshifting seal · **Source:** Oral tradition

Selkies are seals who shed their skins to become human on land. Hide the skin and they stay human, bound to you — a story used both romantically and darkly across Scotland, Ireland, and the Faroe Islands. They are not monsters in the attacking sense; they are boundary-crossers, and the folklore treats stealing a selkie's skin as a moral wound that echoes for generations.

**Where to learn more:** [Wikipedia: Selkie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Selkie) · [Sacred Texts: Sea folklore](https://www.sacred-texts.com/neu/celt/effc/effc09.htm) · [Orkney folklore archives](https://www.orkneyjar.com/folklore/selkfolk.htm)

## :lucide-mountain: The Giants & Old Gods

### :lucide-mountain: The Jötnar

**Origin:** Norse mythology · **Type:** Giants · **Source:** Poetic Edda, Prose Edda

The jötnar — often translated "giants" — are the older, wilder relatives of the gods, residents of Jötunheimr, and the forces of chaos the Aesir keep at bay. They are not uniformly stupid monsters: many are wise (Mimir), cunning (Loki's parentage), or beautiful (Gerðr). The distinction between god and giant blurs constantly — it is less species than allegiance. At Ragnarök, the giants fight alongside the dead against the gods.

**Where to learn more:** [Norse Mythology: Jötnar](https://norse-mythology.org/gods-and-creatures/giants/) · [Wikipedia: Jötunn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/J%C3%B6tunn) · [Prose Edda](https://www.sacred-texts.com/neu/pre/)

### :lucide-cloud-lightning: The Fomorians

**Origin:** Irish mythology · **Type:** Chaotic sea-people · **Source:** Book of Invasions

The Fomorians are the hostile, misshapen race of Irish legend — one-armed, one-eyed, or otherwise monstrous — who warred with the Tuatha Dé Danann for control of Ireland. Their king Balor had a single destructive eye that killed whatever it looked upon, and he was fated to be slain by his grandson: the hero Lug. They are chaos from across the sea, the shadow the gods must fight to create order.

**Where to learn more:** [Wikipedia: Fomorians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fomorians) · [Sacred Texts: Book of Invasions](https://www.sacred-texts.com/neu/celt/leb/) · [Balor of the Evil Eye](https://www.sacred-texts.com/neu/celt/cel1/cel104.htm)
